### 01 ✅ 真实录取
**申请专业：** 未指定  
**申请年份：** 2024/2025  
**学生数据：** 未披露

> As the first light of dawn trickles through the curtains, I smell a familiar aroma that gently tugs me awake. I recognize the scent as my mother's freshly brewed coffee coming from the kitchen, accompanied by the clinks of the moka pot. It is our special morning ritual, one which I rely on to start my day on the right foot. Soon, I join my mother at the kitchen counter, savoring not just the coffee but the moments of connection that come with it. Every morning, that cup of guayoyo (Venezuelan for an americano) reminds me of the power of tradition and community, both as pillars in my life and the driving forces behind my pursuit of learning, as I set out to seize the day. 
> 	The University of Chicago embodies the ethos behind every sip of my morning brew. The Core curriculum promises me an experience not unlike a coffee shop, where a myriad of beans and presses allow me to order the perfect cuppa for a given day. Just as some mornings I opt for a mocha over my usual iced latte without abandoning the possibility of returning to my habits, the Core allows for a similar flexibility and versatility in my education. As I pursue my major, I can take classes on Latin American civilizations without closing the door on cell microscopy labs, all the while I approach topics from unforeseen angles without losing sight of my passions. This only scratches the surface of how the Core will allow me to grow as a well-rounded, lifelong learner, however. By serving up the sweet roast of academic adventure, the Core demonstrates an affinity with what I've learned from my caffeinated forays; no matter the flavor, learning is learning, and through only the most unexpected combinations can I get my intellectual fix. 
> My pursuit of new blends with UChicago won't stop in Hyde Park, however. Whether it's Parisian café au laits or the unexpected fusion of tea and coffee in Hong Kong's yuen yuen, the University's study-abroad centers will ensure that any coffee around the world is mixed with UChicago's signature shot of espresso. The University also understands that coffee tastes best with an eye on the future, as its Career Advancement opportunities will ensure that my passion for learning, whether I choose to express it as a humble barista or as a lofty coffee sommelier, will endure far beyond my time studying at Ex Libris or Hallowed Grounds. A cup of coffee as a UChicago student will represent much more than a quick pick-me-up–it'll symbolize a connection forged over a four-year journey of self-discovery and learning, as well as a promise to keep searching for more after every morning joe. 
> Most of all, at UChicago, I know that immersing myself in what came before won't close my eyes to what comes next. I found this out early on during my time on campus as part of the Emerging World Leaders cohort, when one evening, my friends and I headed to the Quad, where I noticed curious little lights blinking above us. Once the sun set and their glow was fully visible, I realized I was looking at fireflies—creatures I'd only seen in Disney movies and didn't really believe they existed! As we attempted to catch them, I had an epiphany: without turning its back on the past, UChicago opens doors to new experiences, ones I may never have encountered in Panama. It is a place where enjoying a latte late at night while firefly-gazing isn't so bizarre, and where outlandish ideas make perfect sense within the bold search for truth and knowledge. 
> My time at UChicago this summer gave me only a taste of life on campus, one which I want to enjoy an overbrimming cup of for the next four years.  Studying at the most caffeinated campus per capita in the United States means so much more to me than grabbing a cappuccino at its unique student-led coffee shops–it is an opportunity to imbue every cup with the community, perspective, and purpose that makes learning at UChicago unique. While drinking French press lattes, a roast stronger than I'm used to, I remind myself of all the ways UChicago upholds tradition while giving me the tools to conquer new frontiers. My morning coffee and the endless possibilities it allows me to explore have raised me into a life-long learner, always asking "why," and UChicago is the perfect place to continue the conversation.

### 02 ✅ 真实录取
**申请专业：** Economics  
**申请年份：** 2024/2025  
**学生数据：** SAT 1520

> Growing up, my family dinners were marked by my parent's passionate debates regarding political, religious, and even soccer beliefs, where they placed emphasis on understanding the rationale behind these opinions rather than convincing each other of who was right. This led me to many questions: why does my father follow the Jewish religion and my mother the Catholic, and how does this influence their opinions? Why did they choose their respective soccer teams? Why do my parents oppose each other when it comes to politics, and what consequences does this have on their way of experiencing life? Each of their reasons filled me with the desire to attain greater knowledge, allowing me to understand them, but also form my own thoughts. 
> I kept asking them a simple question: "why?".  This reached such extremes that my parents thought it best to buy me a book called "El Libro de los Porqués" (The Book of Why's), in hopes of satiating my endless curiosity. However, opposite to what my parents expected, this book led me to ask even more questions. I want to attend UChicago because I experienced first hand what it felt like to be in a community where people asked (and encouraged me to never stop asking) why. 
> I had the great opportunity to attend a three-week Summer Session at UChicago in 2022. I particularly remember walking into the Markovitz house lounge and encountering a group of students working on a math problem. The same math problem I had not been able to figure out alone. Even before coming into the lounge and seeing what was happening, I heard them bouncing ideas off each other, sparking my curiosity and making me want to ask "why?" As I joined the commotion, I observed eyes light up with different approaches and felt a thrilling energy arise with each new discovery. We were all committed to finding a solution, but, most importantly, we were completely immersed in the process of figuring it out. This was apparent by our eager voices when we shared our ideas and also in the moments where we quietly listened to each other. This time, it wasn't only me asking "why", it was all of us. This is the learning community I want to be a part of. A place where everyone is passionate about exploring knowledge, and a place where I can collaborate and join my ideas with others' to make progress. The UChicago learning environment and community embodies this vision.   
> Although my firsthand experience confirmed my desire to be part of the intellectually stimulating community at UChicago, an unexpected factor reassured my decision. I always knew my decision of where to apply was going to be composed of a variety of factors, but I never thought insects would be one of them. I had never given insects a second thought, until I had the opportunity to hear from Ana Emilia in a UChicago Student Panel. While speaking of the role The Core played in her academics, she spoke of a class called "The Public and Private Lives of Insects", which allowed her to explore a topic completely unfamiliar (and at first uninteresting) to her from a radically new perspective. This singular class was a space where a STEM major, an Econ major, and a Sociology major came together and examined insects, fostering interdisciplinary learning but also great friendships. I can only imagine the insightful and eager conversations that would naturally emerge from my Core classes as my peers and I keep asking ourselves "why?" The way Ana Emilia spoke of insects made me think of the many things we overlook in a day to day, demonstrating that it is worth learning about anything, even areas that at first seem completely irrelevant to us. The Core is a unique opportunity to do exactly that. 
> Professor Leila Brammer in her class "Public Discourse and Community Engagement" said, "UChicago cares how students think rather than what they think" and continuously repeated that students and faculty at UChicago "seek and engage in difference and disagreement." UChicago's commitment to fostering this idea resonates deeply with my aspirations and the type of learning environment I want to pursue as I embark in this new stage of my education. I am certain UChicago is the best place for me to seek answers as I keep asking "why?"

### 03 ✅ 真实录取
**申请专业：** Economics  
**申请年份：** 2024/2025  
**学生数据：** SAT 1530

> I choose the University of Chicago, because I want to wake up each day to a new intellectual adventure. While grabbing breakfast at Valois with my eclectic group of future friends, we'll discuss the Maclaurin series, before scrutinizing the psyche of Duryodhana in the Mahabharata, thanks to our common Core curriculum. As we disperse following our quick bite—some to chemistry, others to philosophy or theatre, I'll make my way to the first class of my day, thrilled that my friends possess such varied backgrounds and fields of interest.
> 
> The lesson makes for an engaging morning period, as I learn about economic regulatory policy regarding illicit substances, before engaging in a brief debate concerning socioeconomic gains and losses from drug decriminalization in ECON 27300: Regulation of Vice. These niche courses surrounding civically relevant, complex issues particularly interest me, as I seek economic policy approaches to mitigating them. Walking over to The Joe and Rika Mansueto Library to complete assignments for my Data Science specialization, I relish the view of autumn leaves climbing up the smooth stone walls of the gothic campus, embracing it like a warm flame. 
> 
> By noon, I hurry to a Becker Friedman Institute Development Lunch Workshop, where I am met with a fascinating discussion regarding forced resettlement and ethnicity-based agglomeration in Malaysia. I wonder what these social challenges entail for the nation's economic development, and how they could be most effectively responded to—perhaps this will be my next big project.
> 
> Later that afternoon, I head to the Polsky Center, where my startup team is fine-tuning our machine learning model designed to predict food insecurity patterns across Chicago's neighborhoods. While I'm there, I feel that this is what I'm all about—applying economics and technology to societal problems, with highly passionate and talented people by my side.
> 
> As the sun sets and I head back to the dorms to assemble the crew for a night out (UChi won't be where fun comes to die as long as I'm around!), I reflect on how the day's experiences epitomize the UChicago experience and why it's the perfect fit for me. With a culture of intense intellectual exploration, a close-knit, vibrant community fostered by a 5:1 student-faculty ratio, vast institutional resources, and a location in one of the world's most influential cities, UChicago is clearly the ideal launchpad for my aspirations to learn, grow, and solve real-world problems.
