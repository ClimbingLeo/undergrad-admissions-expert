## prompt_disagreement (150 words)

> "Describe a time when you strongly disagreed with someone about an idea or issue. How did you communicate or engage with this person? What did you learn from this experience?"

### 01 ✅ 真实录取
**申请专业：** Chemistry  
**申请年份：** 2024/2025  
**学生数据：** SAT 1520

> During a high school debate club meeting, I strongly disagreed with a teammate who insisted on relying solely on emotional appeals for our argument on environmental policy. I believed a robust case demanded scientific data and logical reasoning. I approached him after the meeting, acknowledging his passion but then calmly presented peer-reviewed studies and statistical analyses, explaining how empirical evidence would strengthen our position against counterarguments. He initially resisted, preferring a more dramatic narrative. I learned that while facts are essential, truly effective communication also requires understanding another's core motivation. By reframing my argument to show how data could amplify his emotional appeal, we found common ground, ultimately crafting a more persuasive and balanced presentation.

> In my economics class, a classmate argued that raising the minimum wage would only harm small businesses. I disagreed — not because I thought he was completely wrong, but because the data I had seen suggested a more complex story. Instead of debating in class, I asked him to meet during lunch to talk it out. I brought in research from both sides, including case studies from local businesses, and asked him to share his personal experience — his family owns a restaurant. That conversation taught me that disagreement doesn’t have to mean opposition. We left the table still in partial disagreement, but with a better understanding of the trade-offs — and each other. I learned how much more effective dialogue becomes when it’s based on curiosity, not the need to be right.

I countered that these would render the site slow and unusable for our target users—elderly individuals and those with limited internet access or older devices in our low-income community.

We reached a compromise: we implemented key animations but heavily optimized everything else. I learned that effectively communicating technical limitations requires empathy and data-driven advocacy, not just technical knowledge. It reinforced my conviction that true innovation serves all users, not just those with the best technology.
