> "Think about an academic subject that inspires you. Describe how you have furthered this interest inside and/or outside of the classroom."

## 样本

### 01 ✅ 真实录取
**申请专业：** Biochemical Sciences  
**申请年份：** 2024/2025  
**学生数据：** 未披露

> "If you ever feel sad. Remember: There are millions of cells in your body that care for you."
> 
> These were the words I distinctly remember seeing on my TikTok 'for you page' and words that I've occasionally used to uplift myself whenever I feel down. I used to pair this with the thought of a video I saw in Biology class where a lonely phagocyte can be seen slowly wandering through a pool of red blood cells, tirelessly searching for pathogens. The idea of my immune system constantly defending and protecting me was both comforting and fascinating. It sparked a deep interest in understanding how our bodies work at a cellular level.
> 
> This initial view of the immune system as a source of comfort and protection contrasted sharply with what I later observed in real life. During my junior year, I had the opportunity to volunteer at my local hospital. There, I closely interacted with children diagnosed with Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL), a type of blood cancer where the bone marrow produces an excessive number of immature white blood cells. These dysfunctional cells then crowd out healthy ones, leading to severe complications. For these children, the very cells that are meant to protect and "care" for their bodies were actually contributing to their suffering.
> 
> This realization that the immune system could also be a source of harm was further expanded during a shadowing experience at another hospital. There, I witnessed a case where a child suffering from third-degree burns went into septic shock. The child's immune system, in its attempt to combat infection, triggered organ failure. I witnessed how the boy's blood pressure dropped to unsalvageable extents and the heart rate monitor flatlined. In both situations, I saw how the immune system, instead of being a nurturing force, turned into a source of devastation.
> 
> These experiences have shifted my view of the immune system from a simple protector to a complex, sometimes troubling force. As I continue my studies, I'm eager to explore this balance further and contribute to solutions that use biology to alleviate suffering and improve lives.

**申请专业：** Molecular Biology  
**申请年份：** 2024/2025  
**学生数据：** SAT 1400

> My fascination with biology began in an unconventional laboratory: the blue rug in my living room. Six-year-old me would sit cross-legged, watching YouTube science videos on my mother's phone and diligently recording my findings in notebooks. A general science class in school was not enough; I longed for a deeper understanding.
> Driven by my parents' support of my scientific curiosity, Mini Academy of Science and Technology Peru became my first real laboratory experience. By a stroke of luck, I was chosen by lottery to join their program at age 11, spending weekends learning science on university campuses in Lima. In one session, Dr. Yin from UTMB Health taught us to crystallize lysozyme, a protein from egg whites. I repeated these experiments for my seventh-grade school project, analyzing the feasibility of lysozyme crystallization for supplements.
> In eighth grade, driven by a teacher's recommendation and a desire for real-world impact, I joined FDR-HB Peru, the only Peruvian team in the International Genetically Engineered Machine (iGEM) competition. I joined mid-season, helping design a cell-free system to detect cadmium in the soil of cocoa plantations. Many Peruvian cocoa beans don't meet export requirements due to heavy metals, and this initiative aimed to avoid economic losses for farmers. iGEM revealed the potential of synthetic biology to solve real-world problems.
> In some districts of Lima, pollution is so extreme that mountains of garbage block traffic. Inspired by this, I led my team to design an enzymatic pathway for E. coli to biodegrade plastic into ethanol, a biofuel. To optimize this process, I created a cellular insert that accelerates E. coli fermentation. Despite not testing every step, we achieved a 13% reduction in PET samples in three days, establishing a framework for continued research. In iGEM, I made my first significant scientific contribution and discovered my passion for bioengineering.
> Sitting on the blue carpet today, I reflect on the importance of science in my life. Each experience has been a stepping stone in my development, teaching me to work collaboratively and with determination to make a significant social impact — I know my bioengineering journey is just beginning.
