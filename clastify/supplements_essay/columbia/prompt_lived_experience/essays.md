## CU-2023-2 (150 words)

> "A hallmark of the Columbia experience is being able to learn and thrive in an equitable and inclusive community with a wide range of perspectives. Tell us about an aspect of your own perspective, viewpoint or lived experience that is important to you, and describe how it has shaped the way you would learn from and contribute to Columbia's diverse and collaborative community."

## 样本

### 01 ✅ 真实录取
**申请专业：** Environmental Science  
**申请年份：** 2024/2025  
**学生数据：** SAT 1560

> The first word I ever spoke wasn't just a word—it was a melody. Born to refugee parents who fled the 1959 crisis and the Ethiopian Civil War, I grew up immersed in a mosaic of languages: Amharic, Somali, Kiswahili, French, and later Oromo. My parents and siblings, with no voice, embraced their home again, which was hard. My first day of school, I stood among peers whose Oromo I barely understood. Determined—a language wasn't about to stop me from connecting with my country and my people—I started deciphering Oromo through tales, poems, and songs.
>
> This journey shaped my passion for inclusion. In high school, I founded My Voice, a club for displaced students to share their stories and celebrate cultural diversity. On The Steps at Columbia, I'm eager to contribute this perspective by fostering dialogue, organizing cultural events, and celebrating traditions. To me, diversity isn't just about representation—it's about connection.

### 02 ✅ 真实录取
**申请专业：** Physics
**申请年份：** 2024/2025
**学生数据：** SAT 1550

> Growing up, I never had the option to ignore the importance of representation. As a Black woman passionate about physics, I know people like me are rare in the field. I realized this every time I was the only girl who looked like me in advanced science courses or winning science fairs.
>
> These experiences made me a fervent feminist and social justice advocate. Whether it was through founding PhysGirls, an organization that teaches low-income girls in Ghana physics and coding, or through being a DEI student advocate planning workshops for Women's Day and Africa Day, I took every opportunity to empower others.
>
> At Columbia, I will continue this work in a community that values diversity and inclusion. With organizations like the Women of Color Collective and Women in Science at Columbia, I will continue advocating for equitable representation and ensuring no girl feels discouraged from pursuing STEM.

### 03 ✅ 真实录取
**申请专业：** Mathematics
**申请年份：** 2024/2025
**学生数据：** SAT 1570

> As a low-income, first-generation African American student, I've learned to navigate systems not built for me. I've filled out tax forms without help, taught myself advanced math through online forums, and turned public libraries into my second classroom. This background has given me a lens of persistence—and empathy. I know what it feels like to be underestimated. I also know what it feels like to beat expectations.
>
> At Columbia, I want to bring that experience into classrooms and community spaces. Whether collaborating in a problem-solving group or organizing peer mentoring for underserved students, I'll use my background not as a barrier, but as a bridge—connecting with others who bring different struggles, stories, and strengths. I don't just want to learn from Columbia's diversity—I want to help expand who feels welcome in that diversity.

### 06 ✅ 真实录取
**申请专业：** 未指定
**申请年份：** 2023/2024
**学生数据：** SAT 1560

> One of my core identities is being multiethnic (Hispanic and South Asian). While I was raised speaking multiple languages and immersed in both of my cultures' traditions, I often felt fragmented—not fully seeing myself in the people I grew up with or in the media around me. By sophomore year, I started intentionally exploring this aspect of my identity, wondering why mixed-ethnicity figures were so underrepresented in literature and entertainment.
>
> To develop a more cohesive identity, I founded a student organization called Mixed Roots. We host monthly dialogues where mixed students can share their experiences with navigating multiple cultures. Through these discussions, I've realized that fragmentation is not a limitation but a unique lens through which I view the world.
>
> At Columbia, I hope to continue this work. I see Columbia as a place where I can learn from people whose experiences differ from mine, and in doing so, further strengthen my identity and my ability to advocate for more inclusive representation.
