#### 主文书全文（Harvard Crimson 公开赞助专题，完整全文）

**文书标题：Fish Out of Water**
**Prompt：** Common App（未注明具体编号，内容属于身份/背景类）

> Fish Out of Water:
> idiom. a person who is in an unnatural environment; completely out of place.
>
> When I was ten, my dad told me we were moving to somewhere called "Eely-noise." The screen flashed blue as he scrolled through 6000 miles of water on Google Earth to find our new home. Swipe, swipe, swipe, and there it was: Illinois, as I later learned.
>
> Moving to America was like going from freshwater into saltwater. Not only did my mom complain that American food was too salty, but I was helplessly caught in an estuary of languages, swept by daunting tides of tenses, articles, and homonyms. It's not a surprise that I developed an intense, breathless kind of thirst for what I now realize is my voice and self-expression.
>
> This made sense because the only background I had in English was "Konglish" — an unhealthy hybrid of Korean and English — and broken phrases I picked up from SpongeBob. As soon as I stepped into my first class in America, I realized the gap between SpongeBob English and real English was roughly the size of an ocean. No one told me I would have to conjugate verbs. No one told me I would need to fill pages of my notes with more words than diagrams.
>
> Biology drew me with its ELL-friendliness and never let go. In biology, you don't need perfect syntax when you can just draw the diagram — the mitochondria looks the same in any language. As I colored cells and labeled diagrams, I was comforted by the universality of science. I memorized the color-coded steps of cellular respiration to cope: the electrons rolling down the digestive tract, the rotor of the ATP synthase spinning like a waterwheel.
>
> This used to hold true for my school day, when I constantly chugged water to fill awkward silences and lubricate my tongue to form better vowels. This habit in turn became a test of English-speaking and bladder control: I constantly missed the timing to go to the bathroom by worrying about how to ask. The only times I could express myself were through my fingers, between the pages of Debussy and under my pencil tip. To fulfill my need for self-expression and communication, I took up classical music, visual art, and later, creative writing. To this day, I will never forget the ineffable excitement when I delivered a concerto, finished a sculpture, and found beautiful words that I could not pronounce.
>
> If language is saltwater, I am a freshwater fish slowly learning to osmoregulate. I still can't pronounce words like "rural," because it gifted me with new passions to look forward to every day. Now, when I see kids with the same breathless look that I used to have gasping for home water, Don't worry, I want to tell them.
>
> You'll find your water.

**HS2 Academy 专业点评（原文）**

> Michelle's essay offers the reader a picturesque and witty journey through their immigrant experience of adapting to their new life in Illinois (Eely-noise!). While some immigrant experience essays can come across as predictable, Michelle deftly crafts an extended metaphor using the idiom of a "fish out of water" to connect their passions for both biology and art with their evolving struggle to master English. The uniqueness comes in the candid and often humorous depictions of Michelle's everyday struggles with language, from initially resorting to "clumsy pantomimes" to signal an intent to go to the bathroom to their "ineffable excitement" at finding beautiful new words to express themselves. The essay connects seemingly disparate passions in biology and art to her fundamental craving for self-expression and communication, showing how the reader's defining passion — finding and expressing her voice — has driven her to succeed. This unique metaphor gives Michelle a strong platform to elegantly distill a complex and nuanced journey within the word limits of a personal statement.

---

**案例分析**

**背景画像**
Michelle 是典型的"被动移民 + 主动适应型"学生：10岁移民，语言完全陌生（父亲把 Illinois 念成"Eely-noise"这一细节立刻建立了真实感），没有传统竞赛或领导力背景，而是通过艺术/科学多条赛道寻找自我表达。她不是"精英学生"，而是一个"在逆境中生长出了独特声音"的学生。

**文书核心策略**
这篇文书的核心不是"我克服了语言障碍"，而是：**语言的缺失反而让我找到了多条表达的路径**。每一个障碍（英语太难→画生物图、无法开口→弹琴画画写作）都变成了新能力的起点。这是反转叙事（reversal narrative）的典型成功案例。

**为什么这篇文书有效**

| 维度 | 分析 |
|---|---|
| **核心隐喻** | "淡水鱼进入咸水" —— 贯穿全文，首尾呼应，每一个生活细节都能挂上这个metaphor |
| **具体细节** | "Eely-noise"、SpongeBob English、憋着膀胱不会问怎么去厕所 —— 让读者立刻置身场景 |
| **幽默感** | 用幽默处理本可以写得很沉重的主题（移民艰辛），体现了情绪控制能力和成熟度 |
| **结尾** | "You'll find your water" —— 不是写给自己，而是写给看到同样迷失的孩子，展示了empathy和成长后的高度 |
| **科学与艺术的连接** | biology（细胞呼吸图）+ 德彪西 + 雕塑 + 创意写作 —— 展示了跨领域的intellectual curiosity，不只是苦情叙事 |

**对思博顾问的启示**
1. **移民经历≠苦情文书**：Michelle的成功在于她把语言障碍写成了"找到新表达方式的引擎"。对于中国学生，不应该把困难写成"你们不懂我有多不容易"，而应该写成"这个障碍让我发展出了X能力"
2. **隐喻要贯穿全文**：一个好的隐喻（freshwater/saltwater）可以把本来散乱的经历（生物、音乐、写作）统一为一个叙事逻辑
3. **"Eely-noise" 式的细节**：真实可信的细节比任何形容词都有力量。找到那个只有这个学生才能写出来的细节

#### 主文书全文（Harvard Crimson 公开赞助专题，完整全文）

**Prompt：** Common App（身份/传统/家族背景类）

> My nightstand is home to a small menagerie of critters, each glass-eyed specimen lovingly stuffed with cotton. Don't get the wrong idea, now — I'm not a taxidermist or anything. I crochet.
>
> Crochet is a family tradition. My grandmother used to wield her menacing steel hook like a mage's staff and tout it as such: an instrument that bestowed patience, decorum, and poise on its owner. During her youth in Vietnam, she spent her evenings designing patterns for ornate doilies and handkerchiefs. Then the Vietnam War turned our family into refugees. She continued crocheting after arriving in America, although her ambitions shifted from doilies to clothes for her children — practical objects for a practical new life. As she prepared herself and her family for the realities of their new home, she wanted my handiwork to be of a decidedly less practical bent; among the first objects she taught me to crochet were chrysanthemums and roses. However, making flowers bloom from yarn was no easy task.
>
> Even with its soft plastic grip and friendly rounded edges, my first crochet hook had a mind of its own, like the enchanted broom in "The Sorcerer's Apprentice." It stubbornly disobeyed my orders as I impatiently wrenched it through the yarn. My grandmother's stern appraisal of my efforts often interrupted this perpetual tug-of-war: My stitches were uneven. The edges curled inward. I would unravel my work and start anew.
>
> I convinced myself that cobbling together a lopsided rectangle would be the pinnacle of my crochet prowess but refused to give up. Just as a diligent wizard casts more advanced spells over time, I learned to channel the magic of the crochet hook. The animal kingdom is my main source of inspiration; the diversity and vivid pigmentation of life on Earth lend themselves to exciting patterns and designs. I imagine the recipe in Italian script, the chocolate chips as quick staccatos suspended in a thick adagio medium. But my fingers always stumbled at the coda of each performance, the details of the cookies turning to a hodgepodge of sound.
>
> To create a rhapsodic dessert, I needed to understand all of the melodic and harmonic lines and how they complemented one another. Each subsequent batch I make is a gamble. Will the cookies flatten and come out crispy? Stay bulbous and gooey? Am I a bad baker, or are they inherently capricious? Even with a recipe book full of suggestions, I can never place a finger on my mistake. The cookies are fickle and short-tempered. Baking them is like walking on eggshells — and I have an empty egg carton to prove it.
>
> Though the cookies were born of boredom, their erratic nature continues to fascinate me. Each time my efforts yield an imperfect result, I develop resilience to return the following week with a fresh apron, ready to try again. I am mesmerized by the quirks of each trial. It isn't enough to just mix and eat — I must understand.
>
> My creative outlook has kept the task engaging. Despite the repetition in my process, I find new angles that liven the recipe. In college and beyond, there will always be a reason to return to the hook, a reason to try to improve. I am eager to weave my own mark into the great patchwork quilt that is America.

**PrepMaven 专业点评（原文）**

> Clara's essay seamlessly integrates her voice, family history, and current character into a moving and effective narrative. She starts with a perfect opening. Through vivid, specific word choice (a nightstand of "glass-eyed specimens"), the essay showcases Clara's voice and humor ("not a taxidermist"). At the same time, this essay quickly introduces the subject: crocheting.
>

While Clara mentions the brutal reality of her family's experiences, she quickly returns the focus to herself. It's something many students forget: whatever your past struggles, your essay must be about you now.
>
> Clara won't be deterred by failure, won't quit because something's too hard — the essay shows this about her character without stating it directly. **案例分析**

**背景画像**
Clara 是越南裔美国人，祖母是越战难民。这是一个非常典型的"family legacy + craft"叙事：把一个看起来普通的爱好（钩针）通过祖母的背景提升到了历史和文化传承的高度，同时又迅速收回到"这对我今天意味着什么"。

**文书核心策略**
**"私人手艺作为文化传承的容器"**——开头悬念（"我不是标本剥制师"），中间提升赌注（越战难民→实用主义→祖母想让我做不一样的东西），再通过学习过程展示性格（拒绝放弃+不断尝试），最后把个人的成长和美国身份认同并置（"织入美国这块大拼布"）。

**为什么这篇文书有效**

| 维度 | 分析 |
|---|---|
| **开场** | "我不是标本剥制师" —— 幽默开场，立刻建立独特声音和好奇心 |
| **"赌注提升"技法** | 从钩针爱好 → 祖母越战经历 → 家族身份认同，整个故事的重量在不动声色中被放大 |
| **性格展示** | 用失败-重试-继续的过程展示 resilience，而不是直接说"我很有毅力" |
| **结尾** | "我渴望在美国这块大拼布中织入自己的印记" —— 简洁有力，连接了个人与国家，但不滥情 |
| **细节密度** | 祖母的钢钩像魔杖、动物王国作为灵感来源、"glass-eyed specimens" —— 每个细节都服务于人物形象的建立 |

**对思博顾问的启示**
1. **小爱好可以承载大主题**：钩针本身不特别，但通过家族故事成为了文化传承的载体。对于有类似非传统爱好的学生，关键是找到爱好背后的"为什么这个爱好对你有特殊意义"
2. **祖辈/家族故事要"提升后收回"**：讲完祖母的故事，文书立刻收回到"这对我现在意味着什么"。很多学生犯的错误是：讲了家族苦难后，忘记回到自己。
3. **幽默开场策略**：不必总是用戏剧性场景开场，幽默的自我揭示（"不是标本剥制师"）同样能立刻建立读者信任

#### 主文书全文（Harvard Crimson 公开赞助专题，完整全文）

**Prompt：** Common App（身份/背景/挑战类）

> I swing the door of the dressing room when I text my mom just one word: "Traumatizing!" I'm on a bra-shopping expedition with my grandmother, and just in case it's not abundantly clear, this trip was Not. My. Idea. Bra shopping has always been shrouded in mystery for me, and growing up in a household with two moms and two younger sisters hasn't helped one bit: One of my moms doesn't wear bras; the other proudly proclaims that her bras are older than me. A two-mom family without the faintest idea what a teenage girl needs — par for the course around here.
>
> So when my 78-year-old grandmother volunteered to take me bra shopping, my moms jumped at the chance. Here I was with my frugal grandmother, outlet-shopping among the racks of intimates that aren't sized quite right, that have too much padding or too little… You can see my predicament, and it's no surprise that my younger self was confused by the words "wire-free," "concealing petals," "balconette."
>
> The saleswoman called to my grandmother, "I can help you with her fitting," and I knew exactly what was coming. My grandmother replied, "She doesn't really need a fitting, she just needs a bra." True enough, as it turns out.
>
> But fitting in was my grandmother's worry, not mine. What my grandmother didn't consider is that there's no single way to fit in. I might be two minutes later to class than the sprinters, but I always arrive. I might have to explain to my friends what "having two moms" means, but I'll never stop being thankful that Deerfield students are eager to lean in and understand. I may not be able to eat the food, but you can count on me to show up and celebrate.
>
> While I can't run, I can swim and play water polo, and I can walk the campus giving Admissions tours. My family might not look like everyone else's, but I am deeply proud of my two moms who, between them, have countless academic degrees and even more creative talents and who have always done everything in their power to support me and cheer me on. I might be the kid who shows up to pizza dinners with my own safe snack, but I am grateful for every friend who has asked about my allergies so they can make sure their spaces are safe for me.
>
> I'm not ashamed of the way I am wired differently. I am grateful, actually.
>
> And when my grandmother insisted I didn't need a "fitting," she was right. I don't need to fit in to belong.
>
> I just need a bra.

4. **感官细节密度高**：每篇都有3个以上可以"想象画面"的具体细节
5. **主角永远是自己**：即使写到祖母/外公/家族历史，文书的主语和落点始终是"我"

- **Michelle** 的方式：**主动寻找替代表达（生物图/音乐/艺术）** → 适合有艺术/科学双重兴趣的学生
- **Claire** 的方式：**被动触发 + 找到连接媒介（诗歌）** → 适合有深刻情感经历但一直未被触发的学生

两者都证明：**写文化割裂不是为了让招生官同情，而是为了展示"这段经历让我有了什么独特的认知方式"**。
